Reading Lists

Foundational Rhetoric

  • Aristotle - "Rhetoric" - Classical foundation of rhetorical theory, introducing ethos, pathos, and logos
  • Kenneth Burke - "A Rhetoric of Motives" - Modern rhetorical theory emphasizing identification and persuasion
  • Lloyd Bitzer - "The Rhetorical Situation" - Defining the context and constraints of rhetorical acts
  • Chaïm Perelman - "The New Rhetoric" - Contemporary approaches to argumentation and audience

Digital Rhetoric & Composition

  • Kathleen Blake Yancey - "Writing in the 21st Century" - Evolution of writing practices in digital contexts
  • Douglas Eyman - "Digital Rhetoric: Theory, Method, Practice" - Comprehensive overview of digital rhetorical studies
  • James Paul Gee - "The Anti-Education Era" - Digital literacies and learning in networked environments
  • Danielle DeVoss et al. - "Technological Ecologies and Sustainability" - Writing in digital ecosystems

AI, Writing, and Composition

  • Annette Vee - "Coding Literacy" - Understanding computational literacy and writing
  • Ian Bogost - "Procedural Rhetoric" - How computational systems make arguments through processes
  • Emily Bender et al. - "On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots" - Critical examination of large language models
  • Sarah Perrault et al. - "Writing with AI: Implications for the Composition Classroom" - Pedagogical considerations for AI writing tools

Ethics and Critical AI Studies

  • Safiya Noble - "Algorithms of Oppression" - How search engines reinforce racism and bias
  • Ruha Benjamin - "Race After Technology" - The intersection of race, technology, and justice
  • Kate Crawford - "Atlas of AI" - The social and environmental costs of artificial intelligence
  • Shannon Vallor - "Technology and the Virtues" - Ethical frameworks for emerging technologies

Quick Reference Guides

Rhetorical Devices Glossary

Anaphora: Repetition of words at the beginning of successive clauses (e.g., "We shall fight on the beaches, we shall fight on the landing grounds...")
Antithesis: Juxtaposition of contrasting ideas in parallel structure (e.g., "Ask not what your country can do for you—ask what you can do for your country")
Ethos: Appeal to credibility, character, or authority of the speaker/writer
Kairos: Rhetorical timing; the opportune moment for persuasion
Logos: Appeal to logic, reason, and evidence
Metaphor: Implicit comparison between unlike things (e.g., "Time is money")
Pathos: Appeal to emotion, values, and beliefs of the audience
Rhetorical Question: Question asked for effect, not expecting an answer

Best Practices for AI-Assisted Writing

  1. Start with your own thinking: Draft initial ideas before consulting AI to maintain your voice and critical perspective
  2. Use specific, detailed prompts: Generic prompts yield generic results; provide context, constraints, and clear objectives
  3. Verify all factual claims: AI can generate plausible but incorrect information; always check facts and citations
  4. Maintain critical distance: Evaluate AI suggestions rather than accepting them uncritically; you are the author
  5. Iterate and refine: Treat AI interaction as dialogue; ask follow-up questions and request revisions
  6. Document your process: Keep records of how you use AI for transparency and future reference
  7. Attribute appropriately: Follow your institution's guidelines for citing AI assistance
  8. Reflect on impact: Consider how AI use affects your learning, thinking, and development as a writer

Interactive Tool: AI Prompt Comparison

See how different prompts yield different results when asking AI to perform rhetorical analysis.

Weak Prompt

"Analyze this speech."

Problem: Too vague, no specific framework, unclear expectations

Better Prompt

"Identify the rhetorical appeals (ethos, pathos, logos) in this speech and explain how they work."

Improvement: Specifies framework, clear task

Strong Prompt

"Analyze the attached speech using Aristotelian rhetorical appeals. For each appeal (ethos, pathos, logos), identify specific passages, explain the rhetorical strategy, and evaluate its effectiveness for the intended audience (young voters). Also note the kairos—why is this message timely or appropriate for this moment?"

Excellence: Specific framework, clear structure, audience consideration, contextual awareness
