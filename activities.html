Interactive Games & Exercises: Learn By Doing with Maya Angelou's Poetry & AI

🎮 Activity 1: Agentic AI & Rhetorical Analysis

Like Maya Angelou rising above oppression through powerful words, YOU will rise as the primary interpreter of poetry. Mark the words that carry persuasive power—ethos (credibility), pathos (emotion), logos (logic)—in poems by revolutionary women writers. Then compare your human insight with AI's computational analysis. This is agentic AI: your voice leads, AI assists. Your interpretation matters most.

What You'll Experience

  • 🎭 Analyze Like an Activist: Identify how women poets use rhetoric to fight oppression—just as Angelou did
  • ✊ Your Voice First: YOU interpret persuasive language before AI weighs in—preserving your authorship
  • 🤖 AI as Collaborator: Compare your human understanding with AI's pattern recognition
  • 🎓 Agentic vs Generative: Learn the difference—one empowers you, one replaces you
  • 💪 Maintain Intellectual Power: AI assists your thinking; it doesn't do your thinking
  • 📖 Study Diverse Voices: Frances Harper (abolitionist), Emma Lazarus (immigrant advocate), and more feminist poets

🖍️ Activity 2: Create Your Erasure Poem

✨ Play Your Way to Your Grade: Transform Frances Harper's abolitionist poetry into YOUR own creative work.

📜 About Frances Ellen Watkins Harper (1825-1911)

Pioneering Black abolitionist, suffragist, poet, and activist. One of the first African American women published in the U.S. Her poetry combines powerful rhetoric with social justice—parallel to Maya Angelou's civil rights activism a century later. "Bury Me in a Free Land" (1864) is her most famous anti-slavery poem. (Public domain)

🖍️ ✨ 📝 🎨

Interactive Erasure Poetry Game

Erasure poetry lets you select specific words from an existing text to create something entirely new. Click words in Harper's poem that carry rhetorical power, explain your choices, and see how your interpretation compares with other students. This is creative, analytical, collaborative work!

🎯 What You'll Do

  • Read Harper's full poem about slavery and freedom
  • Click words that stand out emotionally or rhetorically
  • Create your own erasure poem from your selections
  • Explain why you chose those specific words
  • Compare your poem with all other student creations

💡 Why Erasure Poetry?

  • 🎭 Active Reading: Forces you to analyze EVERY word—which ones carry meaning?
  • ✨ Creative Analysis: You're not just answering questions—you're making art from rhetoric
  • 👁️ Reveal Hidden Meanings: Your selections might uncover themes you didn't notice before
  • 🤝 Compare Interpretations: See which words OTHER students found powerful—build community
  • 💪 Student Agency: YOUR choices create YOUR poem—no right/wrong answers, just reasoning

✨ All student erasure poems are saved and displayed in a class gallery! ✨

📚 Activity 4: Scholarly Poetry Analysis

🎯 Play Your Way to Your Grade: Apply rhetorical theory, feminist criticism, and AI literacy to Frances Harper's "Bury Me in a Free Land."

📖 🔍 ⚖️ 🎓

Rigorous Academic Analysis

Identify rhetorical devices, apply feminist theory, examine intersectionality, and critique AI's limitations. This is rigorous, hands-on academic work—YOUR interpretations lead the way.

🎯 Student Agency = YOU identify the devices • YOU apply the theory • YOU critique AI

Multiple choice analysis • View class polling data • Compare scholarly interpretations

📚 What You'll Do (8-Step Analysis)

  • 🔁 Identify Device: Choose the primary rhetorical device (anaphora, apostrophe, repetition, emotional appeal)
  • 💪 Analyze Effect: Determine the persuasive effect (ethos, pathos, reversal, witness)
  • 🗣️ Apply Feminist Theory: Select the feminist rhetorical strategy Harper embodies
  • 🌈 Examine Intersectionality: Identify how race, gender, abolitionism, and power intersect
  • 🤖 Critique AI: Determine AI's primary limitation in analyzing this historical poem
  • 🎓 Scholarly Application: Choose how you'd use this poem in academic work
  • 📊 View Class Data: See polling results showing how classmates analyzed the poem
  • 💬 Build Community: Compare your academic interpretations with peer responses

🦜 Activity 5: Maya Angelou's Rhetorical Strategies

Match Maya Angelou's famous poems with their primary rhetorical strategies and themes. Learn to identify how she uses language to resist, empower, and transform. No two poets wield rhetoric like Maya Angelou!

Learning Goals
